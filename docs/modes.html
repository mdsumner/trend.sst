What this is

Each cell's monthly mean comes straight from the published sums (summed sumwx over summed sumw for the cell and month), so nothing here is re-read from the source. From each cell's monthly series the mean, a linear trend and the mean seasonal cycle are removed. The singular value decomposition of the resulting month by cell matrix gives the modes, with cells equally weighted (S2 level-5 cells are roughly equal in area).

A mode is a fixed spatial pattern, shown on the map, multiplied by its time series below. The map is in degrees C per standard deviation of the time series, so pattern times series is that mode's contribution to sea surface temperature. Mode 1 is signed so that it is positive where the Nino 3.4 box (5S-5N, 170W-120W) loads.

The pattern and time series together are a compression of the record: each cell gets a vector of loadings, each month a vector of amplitudes. That is the classical form of what are now called embeddings, and the baseline any learned embedding of this record should be measured against.

Masked: inland water

Both products carry lakes. Unmasked, OISST's second mode was the Great Lakes, and GHRSST MUR, which covers lakes worldwide, gave its first two modes to the lakes of the Tibetan Plateau. The analysis therefore drops the Caspian, the Great Lakes and Ladoga/Onega from both, and uses only GHRSST cells that OISST also has. Each mode reports how much of its pattern sits in its top 5% of cells, and eof.py flags any above 70%: that is how inland water showed up.
